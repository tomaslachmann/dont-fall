# M6.1 — A Hit that lands, and a fall you can watch

## Done when

> **A well-landed Hit knocks a Character down, and a knockdown is drawn from the ragdoll's own
> bones — so it falls the way it was actually hit, never the same canned way twice.**

M6 built Hit. Playing it exposed two things it did not do, both of which land here rather than
being wedged into a milestone that is otherwise finished.

**A Hit cannot currently knock anyone down.** `HIT_IMPACT_MAGNITUDE` is a flat `6`, against
`IMPACT_STAGGER_MIN = 4` and `IMPACT_RAGDOLL_MIN = 9` — so every connect Staggers, none ever
Ragdolls, and there is no difference between a solid hit and a glancing one. Every Hit in the game
is currently the same Hit.

**And a knockdown always looks the same.** The `Death` clip is played forward for Ragdoll and in
reverse for GettingUp — a workaround `scene.ts` admits to in its own comment, because the rig has no
get-up clip. It falls the same way relative to your facing no matter where the blow came from, while
the physics ragdoll underneath already reacted correctly to the actual Impact and is already
replicated, interpolated and authoritative for where you end up lying.

**Why this is possible now and was not before.** Driving the model from the bones was tried during
M6 and rejected on sight: the ragdoll had free ball joints and no self-collision, so it folded into
a lump — pelvis to head collapsed from `0.75` to `0.07` under gravity alone. M6 ticket 05 (ADR 0047)
fixed exactly that. The rendering half was never the problem; the physics was.

**This supersedes two deferrals**, deliberately and not quietly:

- ADR 0046 said remote Ragdoll/GettingUp reuses the canned clip, "not true bone-driven puppetry
  (out of scope)".
- M6's own "Explicitly NOT in M6" lists that same puppetry.

Both were correct while the ragdoll collapsed. A superseding **ADR 0048** records the reversal and
the trade-off it buys: an animator-posed fall, identical every time, traded for a physical one that
is different every time and can look worse in the tail.

## Checklist

### A Hit with weight
- [x] A Hit's Impact magnitude stops being a constant — a solid connect hits harder than a glancing
      one, scaled from something the simulation already has. **Superseded by ticket 04**: the
      original source was whether the striker was Dashing, which turned out to be the wrong
      design entirely (Dash was never supposed to let Hit fire at all) — it's the length of a
      held charge instead
- [x] Nothing new branches on "was this a good hit": the existing `IMPACT_STAGGER_MIN` /
      `IMPACT_RAGDOLL_MIN` thresholds decide, exactly as they do for a Bump or a wall
- [x] A light connect still plays `HitReact` and Staggers, as it does today
- [x] The striker's own commitment is what makes a hit good — a Hit thrown standing still should not
      knock down, or Hit becomes strictly better than Bump and Dash stops mattering

### Hold-to-charge, and Dash locks everything (ticket 04)
- [x] Dash locks Hit and Grab out entirely while a burst is playing — neither can fire, or even
      begin charging, mid-Dash (the original design let a swing fire from a Dash, which was a
      misspecification, not a considered trade-off)
- [x] Hit becomes hold-to-charge: hold the button, release to swing, with the release's own charge
      fraction driving the same `IMPACT_STAGGER_MIN`/`IMPACT_RAGDOLL_MIN` thresholds above
- [x] `HitReactionPlayer` fades whatever locomotion action was playing the instant a fresh reaction
      starts, fixing the "hit locks the dash animation" symptom directly rather than only avoiding
      it by construction

### Grab's arm-reach (ticket 05)
- [x] A grabbing Character's arms procedurally reach toward whoever it's holding — M6 ticket 04
      shipped Grab with no visual at all (the rig has no Grab/Hold/Catch clip), which read as
      "nothing happened" in live play
- [ ] Live with two browsers: watched from the grabber's own side, the held side, and a third
      spectator — this session's browser tooling was unavailable to confirm it (`ticket 05`'s own
      notes)

### A knockdown drawn from the bones
- [x] While a Character is down, the rig is posed from the eleven replicated `BoneSnapshot`s instead
      of playing `Death` — rotations only, so the rig keeps its own bone lengths and nothing stretches
- [x] Each bone's offset is captured **at the moment the ragdoll activates**, not assumed to be the
      bind pose: the ragdoll's rest orientation and the rig's bind orientation are not the same, and
      assuming they are is what folded the limbs in the first prototype
- [x] GettingUp is the same path — `blendGettingUpBones` already fills `bones` for it — with a clean
      handback to `Idle` at the end
- [x] Remote Characters get the identical treatment from the identical data (`RenderCharacter.bones`
      is already there and already interpolated)
- [x] The `Death` clip's double duty (forward = Ragdoll, reverse = GettingUp) is retired

### Verification
- [ ] Live with two browsers: a Hit hard enough to knock down, seen falling in the direction it was
      actually thrown, from both sides
- [ ] Live with 12 connected Characters, several down at once — frame rate holds with twelve rigs
      posed per frame (ADR 0046's own perf commitment, now with more per-frame work behind it)

## Explicitly NOT in M6.1

Authored death animations of any kind (the point is that there are none) · cone limits on shoulders
and hips, left open by ADR 0047 · ragdoll-driven *gameplay* — the capsule stays the authority for
where a Character is and what it collides with, ADR 0006 unchanged · Power-ups · multi-Round
advancement · collapsing terrain · Skyfall.
