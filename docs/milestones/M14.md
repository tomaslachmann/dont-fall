# M14 — The game has sound

## Done when

> **In a Match on the base race, every Character is heard: footsteps from its
> running, its jumps and landings, Dash, Sliding, Hit, Grab, knockdowns and
> Springs. Other players are placed by where they are. Every moving obstacle,
> fan and belt is heard near it. Each Environment has its own ambience. The
> Countdown is voiced, a Round ends on a jingle, the Lobby and the Round have
> music, and Screens click. Settings → AUDIO sets the master, effects,
> environment and music volumes, per device, live. With 12 Characters, sound
> stays within the voice budget.**

Researched in `docs/research/game-audio.md` (2026-09-17). Decided with the user
(2026-09-17), recorded in **ADR 0087**:

- CC0 Kenney packs, which I download after the user confirms the files.

- Other players heard spatially.
- The AUDIO tab wired up and adjusted.
- `.ogg` only (Safari 18.4+).
- A voiced Countdown.

## Design

- **Presentation only.** Every sound is derived on the client from state it
  already has. No protocol change, and the server never loads audio.
- **One graph.** three.js's `AudioContext` and an `AudioListener` on the camera.
  Buses: `master → effects, environment, music, ui`. Resumed on the first user
  gesture.
- **Cheap spatial sound.** Equal-power panning with a linear distance model,
  never three.js's HRTF default. Your own Character is not panned.
- **A voice budget.** Inaudible sounds are never created, one-shots are capped
  by priority, and only the nearest loops of each kind play.
- **Event detectors per Character id**, like `JumpSequences`, so local and
  remote Characters share one rule.
- **Files by slot** in `apps/client/public/sounds/`, a typed slot table in
  `apps/client/src/audio/`, every file's source and licence in `CREDITS.md`.

## Not in this milestone

- Voice chat and crowd reactions. Their Settings switches are hidden.
- MP3/AAC copies for Safari older than 18.4.
- Sound in the Track builder.
- Sound replicated as events, or any audio on the server.
- Reverb and occlusion (sound through walls).
